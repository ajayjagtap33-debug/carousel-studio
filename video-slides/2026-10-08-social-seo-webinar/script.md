## Tentative script

Written to be read out loud. Lines in *italics* are what you do on screen. During the live parts, talk over what's on screen instead of reading word for word.

### 1. Social SEO (0:00–1:00)

Hi everyone, and thank you to the Tricity Founder Elite Club for having me. I'm Ajay, Head of Marketing at Track My Visibility.

I spend most of my day on one question: when someone asks Google or an AI tool about your space, who gets mentioned, and why.

Today's topic is social SEO. Basically, how the videos and posts you put on social media can help you show up in Google's AI Overviews.

It's 30 minutes, and most of it is live on screen. So you'll see real searches, not just slides. Let's start with one.

### 2. Let's ask Google two questions (1:00–4:00)

*LIVE: type a question from a founder's category. Let the AI Overview load and expand its sources.*

Look at the top of the page. Before any website, Google has already answered the question.

And look at what it used to answer. Videos. Reddit threads. Maybe one blog.

That's basically the whole talk in one screen.

Now let's try something different. Not a question this time, just a brand name.

*LIVE: search "Jira", then "ClickUp".*

Even here, there's an AI Overview. Someone typed the company's name, and Google still decided to summarize the company for them, instead of just sending them to the website.

So AI Overviews are not only for questions anymore. Branded, non-branded, they show up for all kinds of searches.

### 3. Your brand name isn't yours anymore (4:00–6:00)

And this is not just me picking good examples. Ahrefs looked at this last month. By the end of September, 83% of US branded searches showed an AI Overview. In July it was 61%.

Think about what that means for your brand. Earlier, if someone searched your name, they landed on your website. You wrote that website. You controlled the story.

Now Google writes a summary first. And it doesn't write it only from your website. It looks at what the internet says about you: videos, reviews, threads, posts.

So whether people see you as good or bad is now decided by sources you don't own. You have less and less control.

That's why I feel the job of SEO has changed. Ranking still matters, because AI pulls from what ranks. But the real job now is to influence the answer.

### 4. Start with AI Overviews (6:00–7:30)

A quick note on why today is only about AI Overviews.

Yes, people also ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Search is spread across many places now.

But for most businesses, Google is still where people search. And AI Overviews sit right inside Google. So it's the AI answer your customers already see every day.

My suggestion is simple. Optimize for AI Overviews first. Once that's working, move to the other LLMs. Most of what we cover today helps there too.

### 5. AI Overviews cite social 7.5× more than Gemini (7:30–10:00)

So what does AI Overviews like to cite?

At Track My Visibility, we ran a study comparing Google AI Overviews with Gemini: 383,188 citations across 64 ecommerce brands.

The short version: AI Overviews cite social platforms about 7.5 times more than Gemini does.

Look at YouTube. 8.9% of AI Overview citations, against 0.81% for Gemini. Reddit, 4.51% against 1.78%. Instagram, 2.62%, and Gemini didn't cite it at all. TikTok, 1.33% against almost nothing.

Both are Google products, and they behave very differently. AI Overviews is basically crazy about social content.

And for us, this is good news. Ranking a blog post on page one is slow and competitive. Getting a video cited is a lot easier, because very few people are doing it properly. Let me show you.

### 6. My videos in Google and AI Overviews (10:00–14:30)

*LIVE: search "best business plan software".*

This is a competitive category search. And here's my video, on the first page of Google. That's one signal.

*LIVE: search "bizplanr review".*

Now a review search. This is someone checking a brand before buying. And the video in the AI Overview is my review.

*LIVE: search "track my visibility review".*

Same thing for our own product. When someone searches for a review of Track My Visibility, our video is part of the answer.

So two kinds of videos worked for me. One, an honest review of a product, which shows up when people search the brand name. Two, a better version of a video that already exists for a category search.

And honestly, this was easier than ranking a blog post for the same searches.

### 7. Short-form counts too (14:30–16:30)

*LIVE: show the Reels, Facebook videos and YouTube Shorts that are cited, and Google's short videos section.*

And it's not only long YouTube videos. Reels, Facebook videos, YouTube Shorts. These are getting cited too, and they show up in Google's short videos section.

Here's the part people miss. Some of these shorts don't get many views, and that's fine. When Google sees many videos from the same person, about the same topic and the same brand, that's a signal you're an authority on it.

Even if it doesn't bring sales directly, it builds familiarity. So later, when someone is ready to buy and lands on your website, they already know your brand. They've already seen you. That's warming them up.

### 8. You control what evidence exists (16:30–17:30)

If I had to sum up this part in one line: you don't control what AI says about you. You control what evidence exists.

If there's no video, no review, no thread about you, AI will use whatever else it finds. So let's talk about how to make your evidence good.

### 9. AI can read your videos (17:30–19:00)

First thing to understand: AI can now read your videos.

Google pulls from two places, your video description and the auto-generated transcript. So whatever you say out loud in the video becomes text that AI can quote.

That changes how you make a video. You're not only talking to the viewer. You're also writing for the AI.

### 10. Five things to fix on every video (19:00–22:30)

So here are five things I do on every video. Some of these will sound basic, maybe even outdated. They still work.

One. Put the exact question in the title and the description. If people search "best business plan software", use those exact words. In my experience, matching the exact question increases the chance of getting cited for it.

Two. Write a proper description with your keywords. Not two lines. Explain what the video covers.

Three. On Reels and shorts, put keywords in the caption. Hashtags are mostly old now. What I do is add a line of keywords in brackets at the end of the caption.

Four. Fix the transcript. YouTube's auto transcript often gets brand names wrong. Open it and correct your brand name and key terms, because that's what AI reads.

Five. A/B test your titles on YouTube, and keep the one that works.

And one more thing at the bottom here. Since July, Google Search Console lets you add your YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and X accounts. That's Google itself telling you social is part of search now. So add them.

### 11. How to find what to make (22:30–25:30)

Now the obvious question: what videos should you make?

I'll show you how I do it in Track My Visibility.

*LIVE: open tracked prompts, go to the Sources tab, filter by YouTube.*

These are the prompts I track. This is the Sources tab, which shows every source AI used when it answered. I filter by YouTube, and now I can see the exact videos AI is citing for the questions I care about.

Each one of these is a video topic. I open it, see what it's missing, and make a better version.

If you don't want to use a tool, you can do this for free. Write down the keywords and questions that matter for your brand. Search each one on Google. Look at which videos the AI Overview cites. Then ask yourself: can I make a better one?

Try different angles too. Test all the tools in your category, show use cases, do comparisons, cut short clips. More angles means more chances to get cited.

### 12. The 90-day playbook (25:30–28:00)

If you want a plan, here's 90 days.

Days 1 to 30: find your channel. It's not only YouTube. Search your questions, check what gets cited in your niche, and pick the one or two platforms that actually show up. Fix your channel basics and connect your accounts to Search Console.

Days 31 to 60: make the better version. For each cited video, make one that's clearly better, plus an honest review of your own product. Cut every long video into short clips.

Days 61 to 90: re-check and double down. Search your questions again, see which of your videos are now cited, do more of what worked, and fix what didn't.

And the metric to watch is citations, not views. A video with a few hundred views that gets cited for your main question is doing more work than a viral one that isn't.

### 13. Close (28:00–30:00)

Let me close with this.

For a long time, the game was getting people to your website. That's changing. Google now answers before anyone clicks, even when they search your brand name. And it builds that answer from what the internet says about you.

You can't control what AI says. You can control what evidence exists. And right now, the easiest evidence to create, and the one most of your competitors are ignoring, is video.

So one thing for this week. Pick ten questions your customers ask. Search them. See who's getting cited. Then make something better.

Do that for 90 days, and AI will know who you are.

Thank you so much. Happy to take questions.

## If you run late

The four live parts are where the time goes. Check the clock at two points:

- **At 10:00 you should be on slide 6.** If you're behind, in slide 2 show only Jira and skip ClickUp, and say slide 4 in two sentences.
- **At 22:30 you should be on slide 11.** If you're behind, show the TMV Sources tab only and say the free version in one line. Then go straight to the 90-day slide.

Never cut the close. If you have to, it's better to skip the details in slide 12 and read only the three headings.
