## Segment notes

### 1. Open live (2 min)

- Show: search a question from the audience's category and let the AI Overview load. Expand the sources.
- Say: look at what Google trusts here. Videos, Reddit threads, maybe one blog. That's the whole talk in one screen.

### 2. Search has fragmented (3 min)

- People used to take every question to Google. Now the same person asks ChatGPT for a recommendation, Gemini for a comparison and Perplexity for research.
- SEO still matters, because answer engines pull from what ranks. But ranking is no longer the finish line. Being mentioned in the answer is.
- Today I'm focusing on AI Overviews. It's AI built into the search engine everyone already uses, so it answers the question before anyone clicks.

### 3. Your brand name isn't yours anymore (3 min)

- Show: search a well-known brand like Jira or ClickUp. Instead of just their site, you get an AI Overview summarizing them.
- Say: Ahrefs (Ryan Law) found AI Overviews appeared on 83% of US branded searches by late September 2026, up from 61% in July. On bare brand names for 100 big brands, it went from 5 to 63 in three months, and three out of four sat in position one.
- Say: in those big-brand Overviews, the most cited outside sources were Wikipedia (72%), YouTube (39%) and LinkedIn (23%).
- The point: you don't control how AI describes you. It forms an opinion from whatever evidence exists on the internet. So you need to put evidence there.

### 4. AI Overviews run on social (3 min)

- Share your analysis of Gemini vs AI Overviews: Overviews are heavily influenced by social, including YouTube, LinkedIn, Reddit, Instagram and TikTok. Have your source on screen.
- It matches Peec AI's 30-million-citation study, where Google's AI surfaces favored social content more than other engines did.
- The source mix: one answer pulls from a blend of blogs, community threads and videos. Present this as the pattern you see across answers, not as Google's published rule. Citation counts vary a lot by query.
- AI can now read videos. Google pulls from video descriptions and auto-generated transcripts when it builds answers, so what you say out loud in a video becomes text it can cite.

### 5. Proof: my videos in AI Overviews (6 min)

- Branded reviews. Show: search "bizplanr review", and your video appears in the AI Overview. Then search "track my visibility review", and the same thing happens.
- Say: the easiest move for any brand is a video of yourself, or an honest review of your product. When someone searches your name, there's evidence you made.
- Better than what was there. Show one or two category searches where you made a better video than the existing ones and it now shows on page one and in the Overview. Say what made yours better.
- Short-form counts too. Show Reels, Facebook videos and YouTube Shorts cited in AI Overviews and in Google's short videos section.
- Say: even if a short gets few views on the platform, it's still a signal that you're an authority on the topic.

### 6. How to find what to make (4 min)

- Show: in Track My Visibility, open your tracked prompts, go to the Sources tab, and filter by YouTube. These are the videos AI is already citing for the questions you care about.
- Take one topic all the way through: here's the cited video, here's what it's missing, here's the video I'd make.
- The free version: write down the keywords and questions that matter for your brand. Search each one, look at the videos the AI Overview cites, and ask whether you can make a better version.
- Try multiple angles on the same topic: test every tool in the category, show use cases, make comparisons, and cut short-form clips around your niche.

### 7. The basics that still work (3 min)

- Say: some of this will sound basic or old-school. It still matters.
- Use the exact keyword or exact question in the title and description. In my experience, matching the question raises the chance of getting cited for it.
- Hashtags are mostly done. Put keywords in the content instead, for example a line of keywords in brackets at the end of a caption.
- A/B test titles on YouTube.
- Say the topic and brand out loud and check the auto-transcript before publishing, because that's what AI reads.
- Optimize the channel itself: name, description and links should carry your main keywords.
- Search Console now lets you add YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and X as properties (since July 2026). That's Google telling you social content is part of search.

### 8. The 90-day playbook (4 min)

Walk through the three phases in the playbook section below. Spend most of the time on phase 1, because picking the right channel is the step people skip.

### 9. Close (2 min)

Read the closing statement below, slowly. End on the one action, then thank them.

## The 90-day social SEO playbook

The playbook isn't only about YouTube. The first month is about finding which channel actually gets cited in your niche, then putting your effort there.

### Days 1–30: Find your channel and your questions

- List 20 to 30 searches that matter: category questions, "best X", "X vs Y", "X review" and your own brand name.
- Search each one on Google and note what the AI Overview cites: which platforms, which videos, which threads.
- Run the same questions through Gemini and ChatGPT to see which social platforms win in your space. Pick one or two primary channels based on that, not on habit.
- Connect your YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and X accounts to Search Console.
- Fix the channel basics: name, description and links with your main keywords.

### Days 31–60: Make the better version

- For each cited video in your space, make one that's clearly better: test the tools, show real use cases, compare honestly.
- Make an honest review of your own product, or get a customer to, so branded searches have evidence you created.
- Turn every long video into three to five short clips for Shorts, Reels and TikTok.
- Put the exact question in the title, write full descriptions, add keywords to captions, check the transcript, and A/B test titles.

### Days 61–90: Measure and double down

- Re-run your list of searches and check which of your videos are now cited, either manually or in Track My Visibility.
- Check the search terms bringing people to your social content in Search Console.
- Do more of the formats that got cited. Retitle or remake the ones that didn't.
- Expand to a second channel, plus Reddit or LinkedIn if they showed up in your research.

The metric to watch is citations, not views. A video with a few hundred views that gets cited in the AI Overview for your main question is doing more work than a viral one that isn't.

## Closing statement

> For twenty years, the game was getting people to your website. That's changing. Google now answers the question before anyone clicks, even when they type your brand name. And it builds that answer from what the internet says about you: the reviews, the threads, the videos.
>
> You can't control what AI says about you. You can control what evidence exists. Right now the easiest evidence to create, and the one most of your competitors are ignoring, is video.
>
> So here's the one thing I'd do this week. Pick ten questions your customers ask. Search them. Look at who's getting cited. Then make something better than what's there.
>
> Do that for 90 days and you won't need to guess whether AI knows who you are. Thank you.
