# Format playbooks

Pick one that the brand lists in brands.md. Formats shared by both brands come first; TMV-only formats are at the end. Each gives the beat order, a word budget and what the research must find. The beats are an order, not a fill-in template: write them as linked spoken sentences (flow.md). Nick's corpus only covers the first, List, Alternative and Trend story; the others are built for Refluke on the same rules.

Budgets at ~245 wpm: 120 words ≈ 30 s, 160 ≈ 40 s, 200 ≈ 50 s, 235 ≈ 58 s.

---

## Tool review (110-160 words)
A tool, repo, skill, plugin or app people can use today.
1. **HOOK**: outcome + free/no-pay ("You can now… completely free").
2. **PAYOFF + PROOF**: "It's called X, and it's a [6-word what-it-is] that's already [number]." Stars, users, who built it (a big brand beats a number).
3. **STEPS** (2-3 sentences): "You just [input]… and it'll [visible action]… then it'll [output]." Concrete nouns.
4. **ANALOGY**: "Think of it like…"
5. **PLUS**: one extra feature, or price/ease.
6. **CONTRAST** (optional): "Most X… This one…"
7. **CTA**.
Research: README, star count + date, install steps, a real example output, the catch.

## Use case (120-170 words)
"Here's what I/you can do with X", one job done start to finish. Ajay's own builds are the strongest version (only if he did it).
1. **HOOK**: the finished result first ("I just built a booking page that takes payments, in 20 minutes, without writing code.").
2. **TOOL**: "I used X, and here's exactly how."
3. **STEPS** (3): what was typed/clicked → what came back. Name the real prompt or setting.
4. **RESULT**: the number (time, money, output count) or the thing on screen.
5. **WHO IT'S FOR / STAKES**: "So if you [run X], this replaces [paid thing / hours]."
6. **CTA**: comment for the prompt / workflow.
Research: Ajay's own screen recordings or notes. Ask for them if missing; never invent a result.

## Claude trick / ChatGPT trick (110-150 words)
A feature, setting, prompt or hidden behaviour most people don't use.
1. **HOOK**: the pain or the wasted effort ("You're using ChatGPT wrong if you still…", "Claude has a setting that…"), or the surprising outcome.
2. **NAME THE TRICK**: where it is, in words the viewer can follow on screen ("Open Settings, then Capabilities, and turn on…").
3. **EXACTLY WHAT TO DO**: the literal prompt words or clicks. Quote the prompt.
4. **BEFORE → AFTER**: what the answer looked like without it vs with it. Specific.
5. **PLUS**: a second use of the same trick.
6. **CTA**: comment for the full prompt / the list of prompts.
Research: test or confirm it in official docs/help pages; note plan limits (free vs Pro). A trick that only works on a paid plan says so.

## AI shortcut (90-140 words)
Instead of the slow way, the fast way. Short and punchy.
1. **HOOK**: "Stop [slow common thing]." / "This takes 10 seconds instead of an hour."
2. **THE OLD WAY** in one sentence (concrete).
3. **THE SHORTCUT**: the exact steps or keys.
4. **RESULT** with a number.
5. **PLUS / CTA**.

## News (120-170 words)
Something happened in AI in the last few days.
1. **HOOK**: "[Known brand] just [did a specific thing]", and why it's big in the same sentence.
2. **WHAT IT IS** in plain words: one sentence, no jargon.
3. **WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU**: 2 concrete things the viewer can now do (named tasks).
4. **PROOF / CONTEXT**: one sourced number or comparison.
5. **THE CATCH**: availability, price, region, waitlist. Builds trust.
6. **CTA**: comment for the link / the guide / "how to get access".
Research: the official announcement (blog, X post from the company, changelog) and its date. Never report a rumour as fact; if it's a leak or rumour, say "reportedly" and name the source.

## Model update (130-180 words)
A new model or version (Claude, GPT, Gemini, an open model).
1. **HOOK**: "[Model] just dropped, and it can now [one thing it couldn't do before]."
2. **ABILITY 1** with a concrete example (a task, an output).
3. **ABILITY 2** with an example. ("And it can also…" / "Plus…")
4. **COMPARISON**: one sourced benchmark or price vs the previous version or a rival. Skip if there's no reliable number.
5. **HOW TO USE IT**: where (app, API, plan) and price.
6. **CTA**.
Research: the model card / release post, the pricing page, the date. Benchmarks only from the maker or a named independent source.

## List (170-235 words, 3-5 items)
Best repos, tools, plugins, prompts.
1. **HOOK**: listicle ultimatum with the number ("Don't use Claude until you've installed these 4 free skills.") or a problem the list solves.
2. **ITEM × N**: "The first is X, which [one concrete benefit]." + one proof or detail sentence. "The second is…" "The third…" "And finally, there's…"
3. Each item ~35-45 words. Order: good → better → best; the strongest last.
4. One shared theme so the items read as one list (all for SEO, all free, all for video).
5. **CTA**: "…comment WORD and I'll send you all the links."
Research: each item's own README/site, stars, price. Drop any item you can't verify.

## Alternative (120-160 words)
A free or cheaper replacement for a paid tool people know.
1. **HOOK**: "Forget [paid tool]." / "You don't need to pay for [paid tool] anymore."
2. **NAME + PROOF**: the alternative, open-source/free, stars or who built it.
3. **SIDE BY SIDE**: the 2-3 things the paid tool does, and that this one does too (named).
4. **THE DIFFERENCE**: price ("$0 vs $99 a month", sourced) or one thing it does better.
5. **THE CATCH**: setup needed, missing feature.
6. **CTA**.
Research: the paid tool's real pricing page and the alternative's README.

## Trend story (140-180 words)
"People are now doing X." The breakout format (Nick's 116k-like reel). Mass-market angle beats dev angle.
1. **HOOK**: "People are now [doing a surprising thing] with [AI tool], and it's blowing up right now."
2. **ORIGIN**: "It started when someone on [X/Reddit/TikTok] [did the specific thing]."
3. **WHAT HAPPENED**: 2-3 concrete steps of the story, with numbers.
4. **HOW IT SPREAD**: "And now it's a free repo / a template / 2M views…"
5. **HOW YOU CAN DO IT**: one or two sentences.
6. **ANALOGY / SAFETY NOTE** (if it touches devices or accounts: how to undo it).
7. **CTA**.
Research: the original post (link it), its numbers, the repo/template that came out of it.

## Marketing (120-170 words)
Using AI for a marketing job: content, ads, SEO, AI search visibility, outreach.
1. **HOOK**: the result or the problem in a marketer's words ("Your competitors are showing up in ChatGPT answers and you're not.").
2. **WHY** in one concrete sentence (what's happening on screen when someone searches).
3. **THE METHOD**: 2-3 steps, named tools, the actual prompt or setting.
4. **EXAMPLE / RESULT**: one real example, sourced or Ajay's own.
5. **CTA**: comment for the template / checklist / guide.

# TMV formats

TMV also uses Use case, Tool review, List, Alternative, News and Trend story above, with the examples moved to search and AI visibility.

## Search update (120-170 words)
Google or an AI assistant changed how search works (core update, AI Overviews / AI Mode change, ChatGPT search feature, Perplexity change).
1. **HOOK**: "[Google / ChatGPT] just changed [specific thing], and it decides whether [your site / your brand] shows up."
2. **WHAT CHANGED** in plain words, with the date and where it was announced.
3. **WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE**: the before and after of a real results page or answer.
4. **WHO IT HITS**: the kind of site or query affected, with one sourced number if one exists.
5. **WHAT TO DO**: 1-2 concrete actions (a page change, a check to run).
6. **CTA**.
Research: Google Search Central blog / Search Status Dashboard / the company's post first; Search Engine Land or Search Engine Roundtable for confirmation. Label early reports as reports.

## Study / data (110-160 words)
One finding from a named study (TMV's own data only when Ajay gives it).
1. **HOOK**: the surprising number as an outcome ("Only 12% of the pages ChatGPT cites rank on page one of Google.").
2. **WHO FOUND IT**: "[Ahrefs / Semrush / TMV] looked at [N things]…"
3. **WHAT ELSE**: one or two more findings that explain the first.
4. **SO WHAT**: what it means for someone's content, concretely.
5. **ONE ACTION**.
6. **CTA**.
Research: the study page itself, its sample size and date. Quote numbers exactly as published.

## AI visibility how-to (120-160 words)
How to get a brand named or cited in AI answers.
1. **HOOK**: the result or the pain ("When someone asks ChatGPT for the best [category], your brand isn't in the answer. Here's how to fix that.").
2. **WHY** in one concrete sentence (what AI assistants pull from: pages, reviews, Reddit threads, lists).
3. **STEPS** (3): each a specific change on a page or off-site, with a named example.
4. **HOW TO CHECK IT WORKED**: the prompt to ask, or tracking it over time (TMV can be named here once).
5. **CTA**: checklist / guide.

## Prompt check (100-150 words)
A test the viewer can run in 30 seconds.
1. **HOOK**: "Open ChatGPT and type this: …" or "Here's how to see if AI recommends your brand."
2. **THE PROMPT** word for word (on screen).
3. **WHAT TO LOOK FOR** in the answer: named or not, which sources are cited, which competitor shows.
4. **WHAT IT MEANS** for each outcome, one line each.
5. **NEXT STEP** + **CTA** (the prompt list).

## Myth (100-150 words)
A common belief about SEO / AEO that the evidence contradicts.
1. **HOOK**: state the myth as people say it, then "That's not how it works."
2. **WHAT'S TRUE**, with the source (Google doc, study, official statement).
3. **EXAMPLE** that shows it.
4. **WHAT TO DO INSTEAD**.
5. **CTA**.
Only when a primary source settles it; no "SEO is dead" takes from opinion alone.

## Content marketing (120-170 words)
A content tactic that gets pages cited or ranked: page structure, formats AI quotes, distribution.
1. **HOOK**: the result or the mistake.
2. **THE TACTIC** named, with a real page or brand as the example.
3. **HOW** (2-3 steps, concrete: headings, the first two lines, a table, a comparison).
4. **WHY IT WORKS**: what the AI or Google does with it, sourced.
5. **CTA**: template / checklist.
